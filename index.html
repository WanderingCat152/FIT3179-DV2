03

When low access meets greater need

Access matters differently depending on community circumstances. IRSD and households without a motor vehicle are shown as context, not folded into the accessibility score and not treated as proof of causation.

Chart 6

Where living without a car is most common

Circle area represents the share of dwellings without a motor vehicle. The size scale is deliberately restrained to preserve LGA boundaries. The measure provides community context; it is not itself an accessibility score.

Car-free households are concentrated in well-connected inner LGAs, especially Melbourne and Yarra. Low car ownership therefore does not automatically mean low facility proximity.

Chart 7

Four LGAs combine lower access with higher disadvantage

Both measures are split into metropolitan tertiles. Dark purple identifies the combination of lower access and higher disadvantage; original values remain in the tooltip.

Casey, Wyndham, Frankston and Brimbank fall in the lower-access, higher-disadvantage group under these relative metropolitan thresholds.

Chart 8

Car-free living is usually associated with better access

The shaded area marks lower access combined with an above-median no-car share. Colour adds IRSD context, while the fitted line summarises the overall pattern.

Inner Melbourne's high no-car share drives a strong positive relationship between car-free living and facility proximity. Brimbank is the clearest exception in the shaded priority area.

05

What the measure can and cannot say

The final comparison returns to the four domains, then separates the findings supported by the data from claims this proximity measure cannot make.

Chart 11 · Advanced idiom

Education is consistently closer than cultural facilities

Each box summarises the middle half of LGA results; individual points show the underlying LGAs and remain available for inspection in the tooltip.

Median education coverage is 79.5%, compared with 15.9% for cultural facilities. The difference is visible across most of the metropolitan distribution, not only in one or two outlying LGAs.

What the evidence supports

Proximity is uneven and domain-specific

  • Overall access varies by more than 63 points across the 31 LGAs.
  • Education is generally the most geographically widespread domain.
  • Cultural or health facilities form the weakest domain in every LGA.
  • Some lower-access LGAs also face higher relative disadvantage.

What it cannot establish

Nearby does not necessarily mean usable

  • Straight-line distance is not a measured walking route or travel time.
  • Facility points do not describe capacity, quality, cost or opening hours.
  • LGA averages can conceal variation between neighbourhoods.
  • The mixed source years support a cross-sectional story, not historical change.

How the measure works

A transparent proximity estimate

Each SA1 is represented by an internal point. For each facility domain, the analysis checks whether the nearest mapped facility is within 800 metres in a straight line.

SA1 results are weighted by 2021 population and aggregated to LGA. The overall score is the unweighted mean of the four domain percentages; it is not an official government accessibility index.
