How evenly is proximity to essential services distributed across Greater Melbourne, and where does poor access overlap with greater community need?

Access depends on where you live

Facility proximity drops sharply toward several outer LGAs. The overall score varies by more than 63 points across the study area; the maps below establish that gap and show the facilities behind it.

Chart 1

Overall proximity access

The score is the equal-weight mean of the four facility-domain coverage percentages. Melbourne and Cardinia are labelled as the highest and lowest results; hover over any LGA for its detailed values.

Reading the map: darker areas indicate a larger estimated population share living within 800 metres of nearby essential facilities.

Chart 2 · Clipped bin map

Where facilities concentrate

Select one category at a time. Each polygon is an approximate 5 km grid cell; darker cells contain more mapped facilities.

Why aggregate the points? The grid makes concentration visible without allowing hundreds of coincident symbols to hide one another. Coastal cells are clipped to the study boundary. Counts are not adjusted for population, capacity or service quality.

03

When low access meets greater need

Lower access sometimes overlaps with greater community need—but not everywhere. IRSD and households without a motor vehicle are shown as context, not folded into the accessibility score or treated as proof of causation.

Chart 6 · Spike map

Where living without a car is most common

Each line starts at an LGA representative point. Line length represents the share of dwellings without a motor vehicle, reducing the overlap created by proportional circles.

Car-free households concentrate in well-connected inner LGAs, especially Melbourne and Yarra. The spike map provides community context; it is not itself an accessibility score.

Chart 7

Four LGAs combine lower access with higher disadvantage

Both measures are split into metropolitan tertiles. Dark purple identifies lower access combined with higher disadvantage; original values remain in the tooltip.

Casey, Wyndham, Frankston and Brimbank fall in the lower-access, higher-disadvantage group under these relative metropolitan thresholds.

Chart 8

Access rises across car-free household groups

LGAs are divided into three near-equal groups by their no-car household share. Points show overall access, colour adds IRSD context, and the black tick marks each group's median.

Median access rises from 32.6 in the lower no-car group to 42.3 in the middle group and 60.0 in the higher group. This is an association, not evidence that low car ownership causes access.

Chart 9 · Advanced idiom

From disadvantage to access and the weakest service

Read from left to right: the flow starts with relative IRSD disadvantage, passes through overall access, and ends at each LGA's weakest facility domain. Ribbon width represents 2021 population.

Cultural facilities are the weakest domain for LGAs containing 73.8% of the study population. The flow also shows that disadvantage and low access overlap, but not in every community.

05

What the measure can and cannot say

These charts estimate proximity, not service quality. The final comparison returns to the four domains, then separates supported findings from claims the measure cannot make.

Chart 11 · Advanced idiom

Education is consistently closer than cultural facilities

Each box summarises the middle half of LGA results; individual points show the underlying LGAs and remain available for inspection in the tooltip.

Median education coverage is 79.5%, compared with 15.9% for cultural facilities. The difference is visible across most of the metropolitan distribution, not only in one or two outlying LGAs.

What the evidence supports

Proximity is uneven and domain-specific

  • Overall access varies by more than 63 points across the 31 LGAs.
  • Education is generally the most geographically widespread domain.
  • Cultural or health facilities form the weakest domain in every LGA.
  • Some lower-access LGAs also face higher relative disadvantage.

What it cannot establish

Nearby does not necessarily mean usable

  • Straight-line distance is not a measured walking route or travel time.
  • Facility points do not describe capacity, quality, cost or opening hours.
  • LGA averages can conceal variation between neighbourhoods.
  • The mixed source years support a cross-sectional story, not historical change.

Method summary

How the accessibility estimate works

A transparent proximity measure designed for comparison—not an official government accessibility index.

  1. 1
    Represent each neighbourhood

    Use one ABS internal point for every SA1.

  2. 2
    Test nearby access

    Check whether the nearest facility is within 800 m in a straight line.

  3. 3
    Summarise by LGA

    Weight SA1 results by 2021 population, then average the four domains.

Technical notes and grouping thresholds

Map encoding: choropleths use population-weighted percentages, composite scores or relative bands—never raw facility totals. Chart 2 aggregates facilities into approximate grid cells; Chart 6 uses spike length for the share of dwellings without a motor vehicle.

Relative groups: Charts 7 and 9 use access tertiles at 36.278 and 47.606 and IRSD tertiles at 1018 and 1043. Chart 8 uses three rank-based no-car groups of 10, 11 and 10 LGAs. Lower IRSD indicates greater disadvantage.
